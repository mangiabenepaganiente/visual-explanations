Newton’s third law: why don’t equal forces cancel?

The horse pulls the cart; the cart pulls the horse just as hard. Students conclude nothing can accelerate. The pair is real — and it does not live on one free-body. Net force on an object is the sum of forces on that object. Tap the horse. Then tap the cart.

Different objects. The gold pair is equal and opposite — and each arrow lives on a different free-body. Tap two arrows. The chip is true only when both sit on the same diagram.

Cause 01

Different objects

Fhc = −Fch is Newton’s third law. The first force is on the cart. The second is on the horse. Equal arrows on two diagrams do not cancel anything.

Cause 02

External friction on the horse

The horse walks by pushing the ground backward. The ground pushes the horse forward — and that force is not the cart’s pair. If Fground beats Fcart on horse, the horse accelerates.

The Pivot

ΣF on one object

Net force on a body is the sum of forces on that body. Horse: Fground + Fch = mh a. Cart: Fhc + Ffric = mc a. Internals drop out of the system.

Myth, busted

Pairs cancel the motion

They do not. An isolated pair leaves the center of mass unmoved. With an external force (hooves on dirt), acm = Fext / (mh + mc). Ice is the control: Fext = 0, nothing starts.

Model, constants, caveats

  1. Third law (1D). If object A exerts F_ab on B, then B exerts F_ba = −F_ab on A. Same magnitude, opposite direction, different objects. The page draws the pair in gold (--sun2) and every external horizontal force in cool (--cool). Weights and normals are omitted on purpose — they are a vertical pair and do not settle the paradox.
  2. Horse–cart, hitched. Common acceleration a. Horse: F_ground + F_ch = m_h a. Cart: F_hc + F_fric = m_c a. Adding gives F_ext = F_ground + F_fric = (m_h + m_c) a, so a_system = F_ext / (m_h + m_c). The hitch force is F_hc = F_ground − m_h a (and F_ch = −F_hc).
  3. Friction (Coulomb, optional μ). g = 9.81 m/s². Ground on the horse saturates at μ_h m_h g (static cap on the drive slider). Cart friction is μ_c m_c g, opposing velocity; if v ≈ 0 and |F_ground| ≤ μ_c m_c g the system stays put and the cart friction is static (F_fric = −F_ground). No rolling inertia, no slip angle, no elastic hitch.
  4. Isolated pair (person–boat on ice). Only the contact pair. Person: a_p = F_bp / m_p. Boat: a_b = F_pb / m_b. Then a_cm = (m_p a_p + m_b a_b) / M = (F_bp + F_pb) / M = 0. They fly apart; the dashed gold CM line does not accelerate. Set both μ sliders to zero — any leftover μ is an external ice drag and the CM can creep.
  5. “Do these cancel?” True only when both selected arrows act on the same free-body (they then add, and the net may happen to be zero). A third-law pair is never on one diagram, so the chip stays No. System view: internals are omitted; the remaining arrows are both on the system and may cancel there.
  6. What this is not. Not 2D. Not a gait or hoof-torque model. “Drive / push” in horse–cart mode is the horizontal force the horse attempts to get from the ground (the useful external). In isolated mode it is the pair magnitude itself. Drawings are classroom glyphs, not anatomy. Real dirt μ and rolling resistance vary; the numbers are live consequences of the sliders, not a particular horse.
