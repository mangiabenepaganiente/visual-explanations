Seesaw: torque, not heavier kid always wins

The playground myth says the heavier kid always wins — that force alone decides. Wrong. Rotation is decided by torque τ = F × r (weight times lever arm). A lighter kid farther out can balance a heavier kid nearer the fulcrum. Drag both kids; watch left and right torques, and the net τ.

Try thisHit 20 kg @ 2 m · 40 kg @ 1 m. Same torques, net τ = 0 — the plank balances even though one kid is twice as heavy. Then drag the light kid inward: the heavy side tips.

Cause 01

Torque is force × arm

Weight F = mg pulls straight down. The lever arm r is the distance from the pivot. Torque magnitude τ = F · r = mg · r. Same weight, farther out → more twist.

Cause 02

Opposing sides compete

Left weight tries to rotate one way; right weight the other. Net torque τnet = τL − τR. Whichever side has the larger product m · r wins the tip.

The Pivot

Balance when τL = τR

Equilibrium means zero net torque: mL rL = mR rR. A 20 kg kid at 2 m balances a 40 kg kid at 1 m. The fulcrum is the axis; arms are the decision.

Myth, busted

Heavier kid always wins

Force alone does not decide rotation. At equal distances the heavier side tips — but move the light kid out and the story flips. Students who only compare mass miss the lever arm.

Model, constants, caveats

  1. Torque (classroom 1D seesaw). Weight of a kid is F = m g with g = 9.81 m/s². With the plank nearly horizontal, the lever arm is the distance from the fulcrum along the plank. Magnitude τ = m g r. Signed convention: left-down (CCW on this side view) is positive, so τ_net = τ_L − τ_R with τ_L = m_L g r_L and τ_R = m_R g r_R.
  2. Equilibrium. The plank balances when |τ_net| ≈ 0, i.e. m_L r_L = m_R r_R (g cancels). The page flags equilibrium when |τ_net| ≤ 2 N·m (a small classroom tolerance so slider steps still “count” as balanced). Example: 20 kg × 2 m balances 40 kg × 1 m.
  3. Tip animation. Visual tilt integrates a damped α ≈ τ_net / I with a soft plank inertia and angle clamp (~±18°). It is a teaching motion, not a full rigid-body contact sim — no bounce off stops, no seat friction, no kid sliding.
  4. What this is not. Not a 3D playground with bent knees. Not including plank self-weight (assumed balanced about the pivot) or air drag. Distances are horizontal lever arms for a nearly flat board; at large tip angles a true model would use the perpendicular moment arm — we keep the classroom τ = mg r form so the myth stays clear.
  5. Interaction. Drag either kid along the plank, or use the mass/distance sliders. Presets set the classic balance, equal-arm myth case, light-far win, and a typical playground pair. Pause tip freezes the visual angle without changing the live torques.
