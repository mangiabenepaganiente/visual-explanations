Mirrors don’t flip left and right

A flat mirror reverses exactly one direction: the one perpendicular to the glass. Face a wall mirror and your image’s front points back at you. Its up is still up, and its red-gloved hand is on the same side as yours. The “left-right swap” happens in your head: you imagine walking around and turning to face yourself, and a turn is not a reflection.

Cause 01

Only the perpendicular axis flips

Reflection through a plane with unit normal n sends every point to x′ = x − 2((x − p₀)·n)n. Directions along the glass don’t change. Only the component along n reverses. For a wall in front of you, that component is front–back.

Cause 02

Handedness does flip

The reflection matrix I − 2nnᵀ has determinant −1. Reversing one axis turns a right hand into a left hand (and screws, text, and DNA models into their mirror twins). That chirality change is real. Which axis gets the blame depends on which way the mirror faces.

Cause 03

Your brain adds a turn

To read your image as “another person,” you imagine rotating 180° about a vertical axis to stand where it is. A rotation keeps handedness (det +1). Rotation and reflection differ by a flip across a vertical plane, so your mental model blames left–right.

Myth, busted

“Mirrors swap left and right”

Raise your right hand. The image raises the hand on the same side of the room. Nothing moved sideways: its front now faces you. Lie on your side and the mirror still doesn’t swap your up and down. A floor mirror (a still lake) flips up–down, and a mirror at your side really does reverse left–right, because that’s the perpendicular axis there.

Model, constants, caveats

  1. Exact geometry. Every vertex of the figure is mapped with the plane reflection x′ = x − 2((x − p₀)·n)n, where p₀ is a point on the mirror and n its unit normal. Face normals map with the linear part M = I − 2nnᵀ. There is no approximation. The only simplifications are the blocky figure and an ideal, infinitely thin, perfectly flat mirror (real glass mirrors add a faint second reflection from the front surface).
  2. Mirror placements. The wall mirror is 1.0 m from you, and the slider swings it around a vertical axis from straight ahead (0°) to your right side (90°). The floor mirror is the plane you stand on, like a still pond.
  3. “Rotated you” is the figure rotated 180° about an axis lying in the mirror plane (vertical for wall mirrors; your left–right axis for the floor mirror). It lands in the same spot as the image, but it is a proper rotation (det +1). Its difference from the image is a reflection across the plane that contains n and the rotation axis. The readout names that plane’s normal in your body axes.
  4. Axis report. For each body direction A, the bar shows A · (M A) = 1 − 2(n·A)²: +1 means kept, −1 means reversed, and values in between mean the mirror is at an angle, so that direction is partly reversed. At 45° no body axis is purely reversed; the reversed direction is the diagonal.
  5. Camera. Orthographic projection, which keeps parallel lines parallel so the geometry is easy to read, and painter’s-order drawing. The chest letter is drawn in the plane of the shirt, so the image shows it reversed with no special-casing.
