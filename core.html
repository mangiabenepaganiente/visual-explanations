How do we know Earth’s outer core is liquid?

Nobody has dug there. Seismic waves did the work: S-waves are shear and die in a fluid, P-waves are a push and keep going — they just bend. The dark patch of stations that never record S is the proof.

Cause 01

P versus S

A P-wave is a compressional push — it works in solid or liquid. An S-wave is a shear shake. In this mantle, Vp ≈ 8 km/s and Vs ≈ 4.5 km/s, so P outruns S to every nearby station.

Cause 02

Liquid kills S

In a fluid the shear modulus is zero, so Vs = √(μ/ρ) = 0. S-rays that hit the core–mantle boundary stop. That cutoff — not a drill core — is the aha.

Cause 03

Shadow zones are the proof

Stations beyond this model’s ~114° never record S. P still arrives on the far side through the core. A wholly solid Earth would ring with S everywhere. It doesn’t.

Myth, busted

We did not drill there

The Kola Superdeep Borehole reached 12 km — 0.4% of the way to the CMB. Iron is a density / meteorite inference. Liquid is a seismic one.

Model, constants, caveats

  1. Layered constant speeds (this page). Crust+mantle average Vp = 8.0 km/s, Vs = 4.5 km/s. Outer core Vp = 8.5 km/s, Vs = 0. Inner core (optional path) Vp = 11.0 km/s, Vs = 3.6 km/s > 0. Speeds are uniform inside each shell, so rays are straight lines that refract only at the CMB / ICB. Labeled Snell: sin i1 / v1 = sin i2 / v2 (equivalently the spherical ray parameter p = r sin i / v).
  2. Radii. Mean Earth radius R = 6371 km. Core–mantle boundary at 3480 km from the center (depth 2891 km). Inner-core boundary 1221 km from the center if drawn. These are the usual textbook / PREM-rounded figures, not a full PREM table.
  3. This model’s shadows, then PREM’s. A straight mantle ray that just grazes the CMB reaches Δ = 2 arccos(RCMB / R) ≈ 113.8°. That is the S-wave cutoff and the last direct P in this cartoon. Core-refracted P (PKP / PKIKP) in these speeds begins near Δ ≈ 84° and runs to the antipode, so this model has no P-wave gap — P still arrives on the far side via a core path. Real Earth uses PREM, where Vp drops from ~13.7 km/s at the base of the mantle to ~8.1 km/s at the top of the outer core. That drop opens the textbook P-wave shadow ~103°–142°; S is absent beyond ~103°. The PREM overlay paints those textbook arcs; the live chips report this model’s numbers.
  4. Why the simple speeds miss 103° / 142°. Real rays curve because velocity increases with depth in the mantle (they can “see” farther before grazing). A two-speed ball cannot reproduce PREM’s caustics. We compute the shadow of the speeds above and refuse to fake 103–142 as if the cartoon produced them.
  5. Inner core, as a stretch. A solid inner core is drawn because PKIKP (P through the inner core) and later normal-mode / PKJKP work found it. In these speeds a steep P-ray can enter (Vp jumps 8.5 → 11, so some incidence TIR’s). We do not draw S in the inner core; that would need an SKS conversion at the CMB, which is a different lesson.
  6. Iron vs liquid are different inferences. Mean density, the moment of inertia, and iron meteorites argue the core is iron-nickel. Seismology argues the outer core is liquid. Old classroom lines that “we guessed iron, then dug” skip both steps.
  7. The drawing is a 2D equatorial slice. No ellipticity, no oceans, no 3-D PREM gradients, no diffraction around the core (which weakly lights the real P-shadow). Travel times are path length over the layer speed, time-warped ~320× so a 25-minute antipode P fits a few seconds of play. Stations are 36 rim seismometers, 10° apart.
