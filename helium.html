A helium balloon gets heavier — and still rises

Helium is not anti-gravity. Filling a balloon adds mass — the scale reading goes up. It rises anyway because the average density of latex + helium is less than the density of the air it displaces. Buoyancy is Archimedes, not negative mass.

Cause 01

Helium has mass

He atoms have mass (~4 u). At STP, ρHe ≈ 0.1785 kg/m³. Adding helium to the balloon increases the scale reading: m = mskin + ρHe V.

Cause 02

Archimedes in air

The buoyant force equals the weight of the displaced fluid: Fb = ρair V g. Air is denser than helium (~1.225 vs 0.1785), so the push up can beat the balloon’s weight.

Cause 03

Average density decides

The balloon rises when ρavg = (mskin + ρHe V) / V < ρair. Same rule as ships: hollow things float when the average is light enough.

Myth, busted

Not negative mass

Helium does not weigh less than nothing. It does not cancel mass. The balloon gets heavier as you fill it — and still rises — because buoyancy from denser air wins.

Model, constants, caveats

  1. STP / ISA densities. Dry air at International Standard Atmosphere sea level is taken as ρ_air = 1.225 kg/m³ (15 °C, 101 325 Pa). Helium at 0 °C / 1 atm (classic STP) is ρ_He ≈ 0.1785 kg/m³ from the ideal-gas molar mass 4.0026 g/mol and Vm ≈ 22.414 L/mol; classroom tables round to 0.179. Real party-balloon fill is nearer room temperature; the ratio ρair/ρHe ≈ 6.9 is what matters for the rise condition.
  2. Mass and forces. With volume V (m³) of helium inside: m_He = ρ_He · V, m_total = m_skin + m_He, F_b = ρ_air · V · g, W = m_total · g, with g = 9.80665 m/s². Net tether tension (string holding the balloon down) is T = F_b − W when positive. The balloon rises freely when F_b > W, equivalently ρ_avg = m_total / V < ρ_air (for V > 0).
  3. Latex mass. The model skin is a fixed m_skin = 2.5 g — a typical empty party-balloon mass (real latex balloons run ~1.5–4 g depending on size and brand). Skin mass does not grow with fill here; real latex stretches a little and the string/tape add grams. Those extras only raise the fill needed to lift off.
  4. Volume model. Fill fraction f ∈ [0, 1] maps to V = f · V_max with V_max = 0.014 m³ (~30 cm diameter sphere). An empty balloon is treated as V → 0 with mass = m_skin only (collapsed, no buoyancy). The drawn sphere is schematic; real balloons are teardrop-shaped under gravity and tether.
  5. What the scale shows. The “scale” readout is the gravitational mass m_total. On Earth that is what a balance reports. Helium does not reduce mass. If you weigh a sealed tank before and after releasing He into a balloon, the tank loses mass and the balloon+He system gains that same mass (minus any leak).
  6. Not modeled. No diffusion through latex (helium really does leak over hours/days), no temperature change on expansion, no humidity, no altitude change of ρ_air, no string mass, no aerodynamic drag while rising. Hot-air balloons use the same Archimedes rule with heated air instead of helium.
  7. For teachers. Density, buoyancy, and gases-have-mass. Name the misconception out loud: “helium has negative mass / no mass / makes things lighter.” Pair with a sealed-balloon-on-a-scale demo if you can. NGSS MS-PS1 / MS-PS2 style density and forces.
