Newton’s Cannonball

A cannon on a mountain fires faster and faster, and the falling ball begins to miss the Earth. Orbiting is not floating, and it is not escaping gravity. It is falling — continuously — around a curve that keeps dropping away.

Cause 01

Gravity is a pull, not a void

The ball is always accelerated toward Earth’s center. There is no “zero-g bubble” at altitude. At this 500 km mountain, g is still about 8.4 m/s². Weightlessness in orbit is free-fall, not missing gravity.

Cause 02

Sideways speed makes the miss

Drop it, and it hits the mountain. Give it enough tangential speed and the ground curves away as fast as the ball falls — so it keeps missing. That continuous miss is an orbit. Newton’s drawing is this idea in one picture.

Myth, busted

You never “leave gravity”

Escape speed does not switch gravity off. It means the orbit is unbound: kinetic energy ≥ the well (ε ≥ 0). The pull still acts; the path is a hyperbola that never comes back. g at the Moon is smaller, not zero.

Model, constants, caveats

  1. Point-mass Earth. Gravity is inverse-square from the center: a = −GM r̂ / r². Real Earth is an oblate spheroid; J₂ and higher harmonics are omitted. The drawn disc is a circle of mean volumetric radius R = 6,371 km.
  2. No atmosphere. A 500 km mountain sits in the thermosphere; real low orbits decay. Newton’s mountain is a thought-experiment platform so the cannon is clearly above the disc. There is no drag, no lift, no Earth rotation — the mountain is inertial.
  3. Integrator. Classical RK4 on the first-order system (x, y, vx, vy) in kilometres and seconds. GM = 398,600 km³/s² (standard geocentric μ). Circular-orbit energy drift over one period is ~10⁻¹⁴ relative at dt ≈ 0.5–2 s. Paths that look closed are closed.
  4. Mountain exaggeration. Everest is ~9 km. The 500 km height is deliberate so the launch altitude is visible. Thresholds on the slider — V_circ = √(GM / r₀) ≈ 7.62 km/s and V_esc = √(2GM / r₀) ≈ 10.77 km/s with r₀ = R + 500 — are exact only for horizontal tangential launches.
  5. Classifier. Before each shot the HUD chip uses the two-body conic, not the slider ticks. Specific energy ε = v²/2 − GM/r, specific angular momentum h = x v_y − y v_x, eccentricity e = √(1 + 2ε h² / GM²), periapsis r_p = (h²/GM) / (1+e). Will fall back if the conic intersects Earth and that intersection still lies ahead; will orbit if ε < 0 and r_p ≥ R; will escape if ε ≥ 0 and the ball does not hit. Drag-aimed shots between ~7.4 and 7.62 km/s can still orbit if periapsis clears the disc — the general classifier handles this; the ticks do not.
  6. Time warp. Base ×420 so a circular orbit (~94 min) plays in ~13 s. A Kepler-style multiplier (r / r₀)^{3/2}, clamped, speeds up high-apogee and escape coasting. The HUD shows the live product. This is a viewing aid, not physical time.
  7. Two-body only. No Moon, no Sun, no relativity. Ghosts are decimated by point index (never by walking a flat x/y array as unpaired values). The cannon barrel holds lastDir — it does not track the flying projectile.
