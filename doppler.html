Why the siren drops as it passes

The sound is not going faster. The waves are packed tighter in front and stretched behind, and you hear frequency — how often crests hit your ear. As the source slides past, pitch falls continuously through the rest note.

Try thisPress Play and let the source pass the pin. Waves pack in front. Pitch falls through the rest frequency as it goes by.

Miss = 0 m is a head-on pass: you sit on the road. Frequency jumps from the approach value to the recede value at the origin — there is no “closest approach” plateau, and the model does not include a collision.

Cause 01

Wave speed is the air’s

Sound in still air moves at v(T), the air’s speed — not the siren’s. Crests leave the speaker and then forget it. Every ring on the map expands at the same v.

Cause 02

A moving source changes λ

The source lays down the next crest farther along the road. Ahead, crests are closer together: λ_front = (v − v_s) / f₀. Behind, they stretch: λ_back = (v + v_s) / f₀.

Cause 03

You hear how often crests arrive

Frequency is f = v / λ along the line of sight. As the radial speed v_r = v_s cos θ swings from toward you to away, f_obs falls through f₀ at closest approach.

Myth, busted

The sound is not going faster

An approaching siren is not a faster wave. Two-pitch “high then low” is a cartoon. The drop is continuous, and a stationary listener hears exactly f₀ when the source is abeam.

Model, formulas, caveats

  1. Stationary observer, still air. Heard frequency f_obs = f₀ · v / (v − v_s · r̂), where r̂ is the unit vector from source to observer and v_s · r̂ is the component of source velocity toward the listener (positive when approaching). Equivalently the denominator is v − v_r with v_r = v_s cos θ and θ the angle between velocity and the line source→observer. No wind.
  2. Sound speed. Linear classroom fit v = 331.3 + 0.606 · T_C m/s (dry air, near 1 atm). Default T = 20 °C gives v = 343.42 m/s. Real speed also depends on humidity and composition; we do not model that.
  3. Wavelengths along the track. λ₀ = v / f₀, λ_front = (v − v_s) / f₀, λ_back = (v + v_s) / f₀. Identity used in the self-check: λ_front · f₀ + v_s = v.
  4. No shock / sonic boom. The speed slider is capped at 0.9 v (Mach 0.9). This page does not model a Mach cone, N-wave, or nonlinear steepening.
  5. Loudness is not Doppler. Pitch is frequency. If a relative-amplitude number appears in the readout, it is inverse-square ∝ 1/r² from the source, labeled as such — a separate geometric effect. A real siren also has a radiation pattern we ignore.
  6. Light is a different formula. Electromagnetic Doppler is relativistic, √((1−β)/(1+β)) for a receding source, because light has no rest medium. Do not mix that into this sim.
  7. Drawn wavefronts are a finite recent set. True 700 Hz crests are half a metre apart — invisible at this map scale. Rings are every Nth crest (N chosen so rest spacing is about 14 m). Each ring is a true circle centered on the source’s position at that emit time, expanding at v. Bunching is therefore real, not a scaled ellipse. f_obs uses the analytic formula, not crest counting.
  8. Vehicle is enlarged. A 5 m ambulance is a few pixels on a 500 m road. The icon is drawn larger so you can track it. Distances, v, and wavelengths use the true metre scale (scale bar on the map).
  9. Audio (optional). The oscillator plays f_obs, not a recorded siren. Browsers block autoplay; the page starts muted. If Web Audio is unavailable, the Hz readout still runs.
