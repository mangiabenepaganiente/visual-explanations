Why soda fizzes when you open it

The bubbles are CO₂ that was already dissolved, held in by high pressure. Open the can and pressure drops. The liquid is suddenly supersaturated, so gas leaves as bubbles. Shaking does not add CO₂ — it adds nucleation sites. This is not a new chemical reaction at the rim.

Cause 01

Pressure holds CO₂ in

In the sealed can the headspace is a few atmospheres of mostly CO₂. Henry’s law (light touch): dissolved concentration tracks that pressure — c ∝ P. High P means a lot of CO₂ stays in the liquid.

Cause 02

Open → P drops

Pop the seal and the headspace vents to ~1 atm. Equilibrium dissolved CO₂ falls with P. The liquid is now supersaturated: more CO₂ than the new pressure can hold.

Cause 03

Nucleation makes fizz

Bubbles grow where the energy barrier is low — scratches, dust, or tiny gas pockets. Shake multiplies those sites and stirs the liquid, so the same supersaturation vents faster. Total CO₂ is unchanged.

Myth, busted

Not air · not new chemistry

Bubbles are mostly CO₂, not “air.” Shaking does not pump extra CO₂ into the drink. Opening is not a chemical reaction starting at the rim — it is a pressure drop that lets already-dissolved gas leave.

Model, constants, caveats

  1. Henry’s law (light touch). At fixed temperature, the equilibrium dissolved concentration of a sparingly soluble gas is proportional to its partial pressure above the liquid: c = k_H · P (or equivalently c ∝ P). This page uses absolute pressure in atmospheres. For CO₂ in water near 5–25 °C, k_H is order ~0.03 mol/(L·atm); we normalize so sealed equilibrium dissolved fraction is 1 at the chosen sealed P. Opening sets P → P_atm = 1 atm, so equilibrium dissolved fraction falls to P_atm / P_sealed.
  2. Named formulas (same as validate-soda.js). equilibriumDissolvedFraction(P, P_sealed) = clamp(P / P_sealed, 0, 1). supersaturation(dissolved, P, P_sealed) = dissolved / max(eq, ε) − 1. Nucleation rate (classroom): nucleationRate(S, shaking) = max(0, S) · (shaking ? SHAKE_MULT : 1) · R0. Outgassing transfers dissolved → free gas with rate ∝ nucleationRate · dissolved excess; mass of CO₂ is conserved: dissolved + freeGas = total (normalized units).
  3. What opening does. Sealed: P = P_sealed, dissolved near equilibrium, free-gas headspace small, nucleation near zero. Open: P relaxes toward 1 atm, supersaturation rises, bubbles appear. The hiss is headspace venting; the lasting fizz is dissolved CO₂ leaving the liquid.
  4. Shake does not add CO₂. Agitation and wetting of walls create / expose nucleation sites and mix depleted liquid away from bubble surfaces. SHAKE_MULT boosts nucleation rate only. Total CO₂ (dissolved + gas) is identical with shake on or off.
  5. Misconceptions addressed. (1) Bubbles are not “air” — they are mostly CO₂ (plus a little vapor). (2) Shaking a sealed can does not dissolve extra CO₂ from nowhere; it primes nucleation so opening foams more violently. (3) Fizz is not a chemical reaction that starts when you open the can; carbonic acid ⇌ CO₂ + H₂O is a fast equilibrium that follows the partial pressure of CO₂, not a rim-sparked reaction.
  6. Simplifications. One effective P (not a full multiphase CFD). Temperature fixed (warmer soda holds less CO₂ and fizzes harder — not modeled on the slider). Gauge drawing is schematic. Bubble count on canvas is visual, scaled to nucleation rate, not a mole-accurate particle count. Real cans also have N₂ / air traces and wall roughness that set the first sites.
  7. Classroom constants. P_ATM = 1 atm. Default P_SEALED = 3 atm (cola-like). R0 = 0.55 s⁻¹ base rate factor. SHAKE_MULT = 4.5. Outgas time constant ~1–3 s so the meters move on screen; real warm soda can foam in under a second once nucleation starts.
