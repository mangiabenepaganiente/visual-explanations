Do toilets flush the other way in the Southern Hemisphere?

No. Coriolis acceleration a = 2ωv sinφ is real and it sets which way cyclones spin. A toilet is the wrong size and the wrong time. Rim jets and leftover swirl win by orders of magnitude. Flip the hemisphere: the storm reverses; the bowl does not.

Cause 01

Coriolis formula

On a rotating Earth the horizontal acceleration is a = 2ωv sinφ. It vanishes at the equator (sin 0° = 0), grows toward the poles, and flips sign across φ = 0. That sign is the hemisphere rule for weather.

Cause 02

Scale, not the formula

A basin lives for seconds on a 30 cm bowl. Deflection is ½a t² — millimeters or less. A cyclone lives for days on a thousand kilometers. Same a, different t and L. Rossby number says which world you are in.

Myth, busted

Toilets do not reverse

Bowl jets and leftover swirl are designed — or accidental — and they dwarf Coriolis. Flip the latitude slider: the storm reverses; the basin keeps the spin you set. The equator is not a plumbing border.

The real classroom

Weather, not the bathroom

Use Coriolis for cyclone sense and the dead zone near the equator, not for sinks. Northern lows turn counterclockwise; southern lows turn clockwise. That is the demo. The toilet is the myth.

Model, constants, caveats — for teachers

  1. Formula. Horizontal Coriolis acceleration magnitude |a_cor| = 2 ω v |sin φ|, signed a = 2 ω v sin φ with φ positive north. ω is Earth’s sidereal rotation rate, not the 24 h solar day. This is the local-vertical part of −2 ω × v acting on horizontal motion (the f-plane). It is not a force in an inertial frame; it is what straight inertial motion looks like on a turning Earth.
  2. Constants (SI). ω_earth = 7.292115×10⁻⁵ rad/s (= 2π / 86 164.0905 s, IAU sidereal day). Classroom basin: v ≈ 0.2 m/s, L ≈ 0.3 m, t = L/v ≈ 1.5 s, bowl radius R = L/2 = 0.15 m. Storm: v ≈ 15 m/s, L ≈ 1000 km, t ≈ 2 d. At φ = 45°, basin |a_cor| ≈ 2.06×10⁻⁵ m/s² and δ ≈ ½ a t² ≈ 0.023 mm. Storm |a_cor| ≈ 1.55×10⁻³ m/s²; over two days the naive deflection is thousands of kilometers — meaning the flow has long since adjusted (geostrophy), not that air flies off the map.
  3. Rossby number. Ro = v / (|f| L) with f = 2 ω sin φ. Basin at 45°: Ro ~ 6×10³ (inertia wins). Storm: Ro ~ 0.1 (rotation wins). Near the equator f → 0 and Ro → ∞: no preferred cyclone sense, and tropical cyclones almost never form inside about |φ| ≲ 5°.
  4. Cyclone sense. Looking down: Northern Hemisphere inflow is deflected to the right, so a low fills counterclockwise. Southern Hemisphere inflow is deflected to the left, so a low fills clockwise. Highs turn the opposite way (anticyclonic). The weather panel is a schematic f-plane inflow, not a forecast model — no beta effect, no convection, no SST.
  5. The basin is not a Coriolis meter. Drawn spin is ω_vis = k_jet·jet + k_swirl·leftover + a_cor / v. The last term is ~10⁻⁴ rad/s (one turn in ~17 h at 45°) against jet terms of order 1 rad/s. Zero both sliders: the remaining Coriolis spin is still invisible on a 1.5 s drain. This page does not claim a household toilet or sink shows a measurable Coriolis reversal.
  6. The isolated-lab exception (Shapiro / Veritasium-style). A carefully still tank, left covered for many hours so residual swirl dies, with symmetric draining and no jet bias, can reveal the planetary sense (Shapiro, Nature 1962 “Bath-Tub Vortex”; classroom and film remakes, including Veritasium). That is a lab, not a rest stop. Penn State’s “Bad Coriolis” page and the Scientific American drain FAQ are the teacher references. Do not send students to the bathroom to “see the hemisphere.”
  7. Drawings are not CFD. Bowl particles follow a prescribed swirl plus a weak drain; they are not a Navier–Stokes toilet. Storm parcels follow a prescribed cyclonic sink. The rotating-Earth badge is a time-warped sidereal spin so the turning frame is watchable (~20 s per day). Porcelain, clouds, and isobars are illustrative. Live numbers use the formulas above.
  8. Classroom fit. Weather & climate / Earth systems: NGSS MS-ESS2-5 adjacent (air-mass motion, latitude), HS rotation and cyclone sense.
