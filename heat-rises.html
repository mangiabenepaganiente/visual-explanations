Heat doesn’t rise — hot air does

Heat is not a substance that floats upward. It is energy, and it always flows from hot to cold in any direction: down, sideways, or up. What rises is hot air. Warmed air expands, so it is less dense (ρ = PM/RT), and the cooler, denser air around it sinks and pushes it up. That is buoyant convection, and without gravity it stops.

Cause 01

Warm air is less dense

For an ideal gas at room pressure, ρ = PM / (RT). Air at 20 °C is about 1.20 kg/m³; at 60 °C it is about 1.06 kg/m³. The same cubic meter of hot air weighs roughly 12% less.

Cause 02

Denser air sinks and shoves

Gravity pulls harder on the denser surrounding air, and it slides under the warm parcel. The net upward push is (ρroom − ρhot) · g per cubic meter (Archimedes). Turn gravity off and it vanishes: no convection.

Cause 03

Heat flows hot → cold, any way

Conduction follows the temperature gradient, q = −k∇T, and radiation goes out in straight lines in every direction. From a ceiling heater, heat goes down. It just moves slowly, because the warm air has no reason to sink.

Myth, busted

“Heat rises”

If heat itself could only rise, touching the underside of a hot pan wouldn’t burn you, and a radiant ceiling panel couldn’t warm your feet. Say it this way: hot air rises, pushed up by cooler, denser air. Heat goes wherever it’s colder.

Model, constants, caveats

  1. Air density. Ideal gas, ρ = P·M / (R·T), with P = 101 325 Pa, M = 0.028964 kg/mol (dry air), R = 8.314462618 J/(mol·K), and T in kelvin. That gives 1.204 kg/m³ at 20 °C and 1.293 kg/m³ at 0 °C, matching standard tables to about 0.1%. Humidity (moist air is slightly less dense) is ignored.
  2. Buoyant force per cubic meter. f = (ρroom − ρparcel) · g, with g = 9.80665 m/s², in N/m³. The probe compares its air against the room’s mean-temperature air. Positive means pushed up. The resulting acceleration of a parcel is f/ρparcel = g·(Tparcel − Troom)/Troom.
  3. The room simulation is a coarse 72 × 45 two-dimensional Boussinesq fluid (Stam “stable fluids”: semi-Lagrangian advection, implicit diffusion, and a pressure projection that makes the flow incompressible). Buoyancy is g·(T − T̄)/T̄ (the ideal-gas expansion coefficient β = 1/T). Speeds, times, and diffusivities are in scaled simulation units, chosen so a loop forms in seconds instead of minutes. It is qualitative and not an HVAC or CFD model. Real room flow is turbulent and three-dimensional.
  4. Heater and walls. Heater cells are held at the slider temperature. Walls, floor, and ceiling slowly pull neighboring air toward the wall temperature (Newton cooling), which makes a steady state possible and causes the cool downdrafts you see along the walls.
  5. Heat-flow arrows show the conductive heat flux direction −∇T from the simulated temperature field. Their lengths are log-scaled. Convection (heat carried by moving air) shows up in the tracers, not the arrows. Radiation from the heater, which also goes in every direction including down, is not simulated.
  6. No gravity sets buoyancy to zero. Real spacecraft cabins need fans for this reason, and a candle flame in microgravity is a dim sphere, not a teardrop.
