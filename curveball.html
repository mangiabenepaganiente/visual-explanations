Visual explanations · curveball

A curveball curves.

People say the break is a camera illusion, or that “Bernoulli” alone makes air race over one side. The truth is blunter: a spinning ball deflects. Air wraps with the spin; the wake shifts; the net force is perpendicular to the velocity — the Magnus effect. Order of magnitude F ∼ ρ v ω A. Drag still slows the pitch; gravity still drops it. Spin adds the sideways (or down) shove.

Try thisZero the spin: the path is straight on this top-down board. Crank rpm either way — the break flips. Speed changes how much plate time the force has.

Cause 01

Spin wraps the air

The ball’s surface drags a boundary layer. On the side spinning with the freestream, flow hugs longer; on the other side it peels sooner. The wake is shoved off-axis — that asymmetry is the engine.

Cause 02

Force ⊥ velocity

Magnus force sits perpendicular to the flight direction (sense from ω × v). Side spin curves left/right on this board. Topspin would drop a pitch; backspin would lift. Same rule, different axis.

Cause 03

F scales with ρ, v, ω

Sports form: F = ½ ρ A CL v² with CL ≈ 1.2 (rω/v). That is order-of-magnitude F ∼ ρ v ω A. Zero spin → zero CL → no Magnus break.

Myth, busted

“It doesn’t curve”

Not a pure camera trick. High-speed tracking (PITCHf/x, Statcast) measures real lateral break. And do not stop at a Bernoulli slogan: pressure differences exist, but the honest classroom story includes the boundary layer and wake, not a race around a smooth sphere.

Model, constants, caveats

  1. This is an illustration, not Navier–Stokes and not CFD. Streamlines are a prescribed sketch: freestream plus a spin-biased wrap and an off-center wake. Real seam orientation, roughness, and Reynolds-number transitions matter a lot for baseball; we do not resolve them.
  2. Sports-physics Magnus form. Spin parameter S = r ω / v (ω in rad/s). Lift coefficient cartoon CL = 1.2 S clamped to |CL| ≤ 0.25 (a linear classroom fit in the spirit of Adair / Nathan / Watts & Ferrer order-of-magnitude baseball models — not a universal law). Force magnitude FM = ½ ρ A CL v² with A = π r². Unclamped, that is F = (CL factor/2) ρ A r ω v — order-of-magnitude F ∼ ρ v ω A r. The slogan F ∼ ρ v ω A absorbs the radius into the ∼. Direction follows the right-hand rule for ω × v: on this top-down board, positive ω (counterclockwise when viewed from above) deflects the +x pitch toward +y (toward first-base side of our diagram).
  3. Zero spin → zero Magnus deflection. ω = 0 ⇒ S = 0 ⇒ CL = 0 ⇒ FM = 0. The ghost path and the live path coincide. Flip the sign of rpm and the signed force and plate break flip. That is the self-check built into the sliders.
  4. Flight model on the board. Constant speed v along the mound–plate line for timing (t = L/v with L = 18.44 m). Lateral acceleration a = FM/m held constant in magnitude (direction always perpendicular to the instantaneous velocity in a simple 2D integrator). Gravity and quadratic drag are omitted on the top-down board so the Magnus break is readable alone; both are huge in a real pitch. Plate deflection readout uses the integrated path’s lateral offset at x = L.
  5. Bernoulli is not banned — slogans are. Faster local flow sits at lower pressure along a streamline (steady, inviscid, incompressible). That language can describe part of the pressure field. It is not a complete explanation by itself: viscosity, the boundary layer, and asymmetric separation set where the wake sits. Do not teach “air races over the longer path.” Teach “spin moves the wake; net force is sideways.”
  6. Camera myth. Video can exaggerate break with lens foreshortening, but optical tracking systems measure real horizontal and vertical movement relative to a no-spin (or spinless reference) trajectory. Curveballs curve. Knuckleballs dance for a different reason (asymmetric seam wake with almost no spin).
  7. Units on the sliders. Speed is mph for baseball feel; internal SI uses 1 mph = 0.44704 m/s. Spin is rpm; ω = rpm · 2π/60. Readouts show both human and SI where it helps teachers check homework.
