Foggy mirror = water leaving the air

The fog on a cold bathroom mirror is not cold leaking through the glass, not fog coming through the mirror, and not “coldness turning into water.” Water vapor is already in the warm moist air. When that vapor meets a surface colder than the dew point, it leaves the gas and becomes liquid droplets on the glass.

Cause 01

Vapor was already there

Warm shower air holds invisible H₂O molecules as a gas. Relative humidity says how close that air is to saturation at its temperature — not how much liquid is “hiding.”

Cause 02

Dew point is the gate

The dew point T_dew is the temperature where that same moisture would saturate. Cool a surface below T_dew and the air next to it cannot hold all its vapor — liquid appears.

Cause 03

Glass only sets the surface T

A cold mirror or iced drink wall is a heat sink. It does not invent water. Raise the glass above T_dew and new fog stops; warm dry air never fogs a warm glass.

Myth, busted

Nothing comes through the glass

Osborne & Cosgrove interviews: kids say fog “comes through” or that “coldness turns into water.” Toggle the myth chip — arrows from behind the mirror never add droplets. The water was on the air side all along.

Model, constants, caveats

  1. Dew point from T and RH (Magnus / Tetens style). Saturation vapor pressure over liquid water uses the August–Roche–Magnus form e_s(T) = 6.1094 · exp(a T / (b + T)) with a = 17.625, b = 243.04 °C (hPa, T in °C). Dew point: γ = ln(RH/100) + a T_air/(b + T_air), then T_dew = b γ / (a − γ). Coefficients and the closed-form dew-point inversion are those recommended by Lawrence, M. G. (2005), “The Relationship between Relative Humidity and the Dewpoint Temperature in Moist Air,” Bull. Amer. Meteor. Soc. 86, 225–233 (building on Magnus 1844; Tetens 1930; Alduchov & Eskridge 1996). This page uses that equation and no other psychrometric chart.
  2. Condensation rule in the toy. Droplets stick on the mirror only when T_glass < T_dew. Fog opacity rises with undershoot T_dew − T_glass (full cartoon fog by ~8 °C). When T_glass ≥ T_dew, new sticks stop and existing fog clears — matching “warm the mirror with your hand.” Absolute humidity is held by the RH and T_air pair; we do not model mixing ratios changing from steam jets separately.
  3. What this is not. No nucleation kinetics, no filmwise vs dropwise regimes, no latent-heat feedback on glass temperature, no boundary-layer CFD. Glass T is a slider (as if the wall is a large heat bath). Particle dots are countable classroom glyphs, not molecular dynamics. Pressure is fixed near 1 atm; the Magnus fit is for liquid water, not ice.
  4. Misconception sources. Osborne, R. J. & Cosgrove, M. M. (1983), “Children’s conceptions of the changes of state of water,” J. Res. Sci. Teach. 20, 825–838 — “through the glass,” “coldness comes through,” recombination myths. Beyond Penguins (Ohio State) condensation probes; RSC Education changes-of-state interviews; NZCER ARB “What is happening II?” (~20% of Year 10 still choose “through the glass”).
  5. Drawings are cartoons. The bathroom scene and cross-section gate are schematic. Myth-mode arrows from behind the glass are pedagogical falsehoods drawn on purpose; they never feed the fog counter. Clear the chip to restore the air-side-only truth.
